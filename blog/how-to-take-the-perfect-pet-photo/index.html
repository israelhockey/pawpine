How to take a photo of your pet that makes a great portrait

Simple phone-camera tips for a sharp, well-lit pet photo that turns into a beautiful custom portrait.

You don't need a professional camera for a great portrait. A phone photo works well as long as your pet's face is sharp and well lit. Here's how to get one in a few minutes.

Use daylight

Stand your pet near a window or take them outside on a bright, overcast day. Soft daylight shows fur color and eye detail far better than indoor lamps or flash.

Get down to their eye level

Crouch or sit on the floor so the camera is level with your pet's eyes. Photos taken from above make heads look large and bodies small.

Focus on the eyes

Tap your phone screen on your pet's eyes before you take the photo. If the eyes are sharp, the portrait will feel alive.

Get their attention

Hold a treat or squeaky toy just above your phone. You'll get alert ears and a direct look instead of the back of a head.

Leave a little room

Fill most of the frame with your pet, but don't crop off ears or the top of their head. We need a little space around them to paint the background.

Take a lot of photos

  • Use burst mode and pick the best one
  • Avoid heavy filters; we'll handle the style
  • Choose the original photo, not a screenshot, for the best detail
